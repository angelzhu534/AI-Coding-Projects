Your role at IPX Power

What IPX Power does

IPX Power develops, owns, and operates utility-scale solar and battery storage projects. Every project moves through a defined lifecycle: development → permitting → financing → construction → commercial operation → long-term O&M. Legal ops touches every single stage through contracts.

Your role as Legal Ops Specialist

You own the operational layer of every contract — not the legal substance. Attorneys make legal decisions; you make sure nothing stalls, gets lost, or misses a deadline.

The core mandate: prevent bottlenecks, track spending, and ensure every executed agreement is in Evisort with obligation dates entered before you close a matter.

Golden rule: No work may begin and no invoices may be processed until a fully executed agreement is in place. A pending DocuSign signature is not an executed contract.

Evisort rule: If it's not in Evisort with obligation dates entered, it doesn't exist from a legal ops perspective.

The 7-step contract lifecycle

Business teams should never go directly to attorneys. All contract requests route through legal ops first. This is how you maintain visibility across all active agreements.

1
Contract intake Evisort
Business team submits via Evisort intake form or Slack. You identify contract type, confirm requesting party, counterparty, estimated value, and target execution date. Check if a template exists or if it's counterparty paper. Create the matter in Evisort and assign to the right attorney.
2
Documentation collection Google Drive Evisort
For PPAs: capacity, location, COD target, FERC status, CAISO/ERCOT interconnection status. For land: legal property description, title search, landowner contact. For vendors/outside counsel: W-9, certificate of insurance, scope of work, signatory info, conflict check.
3
Attorney review & negotiation Google Docs
Attorney-owned. Your role: track status in Evisort, follow up if stalled, coordinate scheduling of negotiation calls, maintain version control (tag each redline with date and party), flag delays to the business team vs SLA. Do not participate in substantive legal decisions.
4
Internal approval & signing authority check Evisort
Confirm contract value → identify required signatories per the signing authority matrix. Route in Evisort and notify approvers via Slack with a deadline. Follow up after 48 hours. Escalate to Janelle if unresponsive beyond 5 days. Confirm Finance has approved budget implications.
5
Execution via DocuSign DocuSign
Create the DocuSign envelope with the final approved version. Route in correct order: internal signatories first, then counterparty. Confirm counterparty name, title, and email match the agreement. Monitor daily — follow up on any signatory pending more than 48 hours. Download and store immediately upon completion.
6
Storage, tagging & obligation entry Evisort
Upload to Evisort — tag with contract type, counterparty, project, and value. Enter all key dates: effective date, expiration, renewal windows, COD, milestones. Set automated alerts at 180/90/60/30 days. Notify the business team. Flag quarterly lender reporting for project finance deals. Copy to Relativity if litigation-relevant.
7
Ongoing obligation tracking Evisort Slack
Push information proactively — Finance, Development, and Commercial should never need to chase you for contract status. PPA CODs flagged at 180/90/60/30 days. Land lease renewals flagged 12 months out. Project finance covenant compliance tracked quarterly. Build Slack alerts so teams get what they need automatically.

Contract types you'll manage

High risk

Power Purchase Agreement (PPA)

Long-term commitment to deliver power to a utility or corporate buyer. 15–20 year terms with performance milestones, capacity guarantees, and liquidated damages.

Track: Commercial Operation Date (COD), capacity guarantees, availability requirements, augmentation triggers, renewal windows.

Missed COD = automatic liquidated damages. Flag at 180/90/60/30 days.

High risk

Land Lease / Easement

Long-term lease of hundreds of acres from private landowners. Often 25–40 year terms with renewal options.

Track: Renewal option windows, rent escalation schedules, amendment history, construction commencement dates, access rights.

Missed renewal window = loss of project site. Flag 12 months out — not just 180 days.

High risk

Interconnection Agreement (CAISO / ERCOT)

Governs how IPX connects solar/battery projects to the grid. Defines cost-sharing for grid upgrades and technical requirements.

Track: Queue position milestones, study completion dates, cost allocation deadlines, execution windows.

Missed milestone = lost queue position — often unrecoverable.

Medium-high risk

EPC Contract (Engineering, Procurement & Construction)

Contract with the companies that physically build IPX's solar and battery projects. Defines scope, schedule, price, and performance guarantees.

Track: COD milestone, liquidated damages provisions, warranty periods, change order log, performance testing schedule.

High risk

Project Finance Agreement

Complex debt and tax equity financing — often hundreds of millions of dollars. Governed by loan/credit agreements with lenders and institutional investors.

Track: Quarterly lender reporting, covenant compliance, draw conditions, insurance requirements, maturity dates.

Covenant breach = technical default. Quarterly reporting is mandatory — Finance begins prep at 90 days.

Medium risk

Outside Counsel / Vendor Agreements

Engagement letters with law firms, vendor agreements with consultants and service providers. Governs scope, rates, billing guidelines, and conflict waivers.

Track: Billing guideline compliance, budget vs actuals per matter, rate agreement renewals, invoice review cadence.

Invoices are audited and paid through SAP Concur. You enforce billing guidelines — no admin charges, no unapproved travel.

SLAs — your turnaround commitments

These are the commitments you make to business teams. Know them cold.

Contract type Intake → attorney Review turnaround Execution target
NDA / Confidentiality Agreement Same day 2–3 business days 3–5 business days
Vendor / Service Agreement 1 business day 5–7 business days 7–10 business days
Outside Counsel Engagement 1 business day 3–5 business days 5–7 business days
O&M Agreement 1 business day 10–15 business days 15–20 business days
EPC Contract 1 business day TBD by complexity Coordinate with attorney
PPA / Land Lease / Project Finance Same day TBD by complexity Coordinate with attorney

NDAs and PPAs both require same-day intake-to-attorney routing. NDAs are the fastest contract to execute (3–5 business days total). Complex deals like PPAs and project finance have no fixed execution target — coordinate directly with the attorney.

Industry glossary

COD — Commercial Operation Date
When a solar/battery project starts delivering power commercially. Missing this date in a PPA triggers automatic liquidated damages. The single most important deadline you will track.
PPA — Power Purchase Agreement
Long-term contract (15–20 years) committing IPX to deliver power to a buyer — a utility or corporate off-taker. The core commercial contract for every project.
EPC — Engineering, Procurement & Construction
The contract type for companies that physically build IPX's projects. EPC contractors are responsible for construction timelines and performance guarantees.
CAISO — California Independent System Operator
The grid operator for most of California. IPX projects in CA interconnect through CAISO's queue process. Missing queue milestones = lost position, often unrecoverable.
ERCOT — Electric Reliability Council of Texas
The Texas grid operator. IPX projects in TX interconnect through ERCOT. Same queue dynamics as CAISO.
Liquidated damages (LD)
Pre-agreed damages in a contract for specific failures — most commonly missing a COD. They're large, automatic, and don't require proving actual harm. This is why every deadline gets the full alert treatment.
FERC — Federal Energy Regulatory Commission
The federal regulator for wholesale electricity markets. IPX must maintain market-based rate authorization. FERC regulatory filings trigger at 90 days out.
Tax equity
A form of project finance where institutional investors provide capital in exchange for federal tax credits (ITC/PTC) generated by the solar project. Drives the project finance structure.
ITC / PTC — Investment Tax Credit / Production Tax Credit
Federal tax credits under the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) that make solar and battery projects financially viable. The reason tax equity investors participate in project finance.
Covenant
A binding obligation in a project finance agreement — for example, maintaining certain financial ratios. A covenant breach = technical default, which can accelerate repayment of the entire loan.
CLM — Contract Lifecycle Management
The software category Evisort belongs to. Covers the full contract journey from intake through obligation tracking and renewal management.
FEOC — Foreign Entity of Concern
An IRA-related compliance requirement affecting supply chain documentation for projects claiming ITC/PTC credits. FEOC documentation gaps trigger escalation at 60 days out.
O&M — Operations & Maintenance
The long-term contract governing ongoing operation of a solar/battery project after construction. 10–15 business day review turnaround, 15–20 days to execution.

Real-world scenarios

Practice applying the process to situations you'll actually encounter on the job.

A CAISO developer Slacks you: "Hey, can you send me a quick NDA for a new landowner meeting tomorrow?"
Do not handle this in Slack. Respond: "Got it — please submit through the Evisort intake form so I can get it tracked and routed properly. I'll get it back to you same day." NDA SLA is same-day intake, 3–5 business days to execution.
An outside law firm submits an invoice in Concur that includes a line for "document organization and filing" at $2,000.
Reject or flag the line item. Billing guidelines prohibit basic administrative work like document organization. This is exactly what Concur enforcement looks like in practice.
A $350,000 vendor contract comes in for execution. The requesting Director says "I've already approved this, just send it to DocuSign."
A $350K contract requires GC + CFO or COO signatures (2 signatures). The Director alone does not have authority at this value. Route for correct approvals in Evisort before creating the DocuSign envelope.
You upload an executed PPA to Evisort. What are the minimum required steps before you can close out this matter?
Tag with contract type, counterparty, project, and value. Enter all key dates: effective date, expiration, COD, renewal windows, all milestones. Set automated alerts at 180/90/60/30 days. Notify the business team. Also flag the COD to Operations at 180 days. If litigation-adjacent, copy to Relativity.
An attorney negotiation call has been ongoing for 3 weeks. The business team is asking you for a status update.
Check the SLA for that contract type. If review is running over, flag the delay to the business team and check in with the attorney. Update the status in Evisort. You don't participate in the negotiation — but stalls without visibility are your problem to surface.
A signatory in a DocuSign envelope has been pending for 5 days and hasn't signed.
You should have followed up after 48 hours. At 5 days, escalate to Janelle. Meanwhile, no work should have started on the project — a pending signature is not an executed contract.
Evisort sends you a 180-day alert on a land lease renewal window. What do you do?
Notify the relevant attorney and the Operations/Development team immediately. Land lease renewals must be flagged at 12 months out — so if 180 days is your first alert, that's already late for land leases specifically. For all other contract types, 180 days is the correct early-action trigger.
Finance asks you how much IPX spent on outside counsel for the Darden project last quarter.
This is answerable from Concur — if you've been coding invoices correctly to the Darden project code. Pull the project-tagged spend from Concur and provide it to Finance. This is exactly why accurate invoice coding matters.
