A First-Time Plant Parent's Guide

Repotting,
Without the Guesswork

Everything you need to move a plant into a bigger home — what to look for, what drainage actually requires, and how to do it without hurting the roots.

01 — Timing

Is it actually time?

Most plants prefer being a little snug. Only repot if you spot two or more of these.

🪴Roots poking out of the drainage holes
🌀Roots circling tightly at the soil surface
💧Water runs straight through, soil stays dry
🌱Growth has stalled or leaves are shrinking
02 — Drainage

What's actually happening at the bottom

Here's the part most guides get wrong — the layer at the bottom doesn't need to be pebbles. It just needs to let water out and keep soil in.

POT CROSS-SECTION — soil sits directly on a thin cover (paper towel, mesh, or nothing) placed only over the hole itself, not the whole base.

Skip the pebbles. A gravel layer doesn't improve drainage — it raises the water table closer to the roots, so soil can actually stay wetter longer. The hole is what matters, not what's above it.

No coffee filter on hand? Any of these work just as well:

Paper towel Window screen scrap Mesh produce bag Folded newspaper A single dry leaf Nothing at all
03 — Process

The repotting sequence

Do these in order — each step protects the roots for the next one.

1

Water the day before

Hydrated soil holds together better and slides out of the pot with less root damage.

2

Prep the new pot

Only 1–2 inches larger in diameter than the current one. Add your drainage cover, then a base layer of fresh soil.

Too big a jump in pot size means excess soil holds water the roots can't reach yet.

3

Ease the plant out

Tip the pot sideways, gently squeeze plastic pots, and lift from the base of the stem — never by the leaves.

4

Loosen and trim roots

Gently tease apart any circling roots. Snip mushy, black, or clearly dead roots with clean scissors.

5

Set and fill

Place the plant so the stem base sits at the same depth as before. Fill in around it, pressing gently — not packed tight.

6

Water it in

Water until it runs from the drainage hole. This settles soil around the roots and closes air gaps.

Hold off on fertilizer for 2–4 weeks while the plant recovers.

05 — After Care

If something looks off

Yellowing leaves
Likely overwatering during recovery — let the soil dry out more between waterings.
Wilting badly
Possible root damage from the move. Keep in low light and stable humidity for a few days.
Soil staying wet
Pot may be oversized for the root system, or the drainage hole is blocked.
A few dropped leaves
Normal stress response. Give it 1–2 weeks before worrying.

WHEN IN DOUBT — UNDER-POT AND UNDER-WATER A RECOVERING PLANT, NOT THE OTHER WAY AROUND
